About the Lab

The Bioelectronics Lab will develop flexible neurotechnologies for bioelectronic medicine, in which implantable devices modulate the nervous system to treat conditions such as neurodegeneration, refractory epilepsy and chronic pain. The lab will also pursue neurotechnologies for cancer therapy, building on the growing understanding of how the nervous system influences tumour progression. The lab's work will be organised into three thrust areas.

Thrust 01

Neurotechnologies for cancer neuroscience

The lab will develop multi-well and multi-electrode array platforms tuned for the specific needs of advanced in vitro models, such as brain organoids and neuron–tumour co-cultures. These will serve as advanced tools for neuroscience and cancer neuroscience research. The lab will also develop neurotechnologies that target the nervous system's role in tumour progression, as new routes to cancer therapy.

Thrust 02

Thin-film implants for clinical translation

The lab will address the barriers that keep flexible polymer thin-film implants (polyimide, parylene C) out of the clinic. These films offer far better mechanical compliance with neural tissue than rigid devices and therefore superior long-term stability and integration. The lab will (a) develop high-density interconnects and thin-film front-end electronics, using organic electrochemical transistors, to couple soft probes to rigid backends and reduce wiring complexity; and (b) systematically study failure mechanisms and chronic stability under accelerated aging.

Thrust 03

Biohybrid neural implants

In the long term, the lab will pursue biohybrid neural implants that combine living tissue with electronics for seamless integration with the host nervous system.

For Prospective PhD Students

The lab is looking for motivated PhD students. If you are interested in joining, here is what I look for and what you can expect. This section will be updated as the lab grows.

What I Expect From Incoming PhD Students

  • Broad research interests aligned with the group's focus on bioelectronics: developing neurotechnologies, biosensors, wearable sensors and implantable medical devices.
  • Openness to collaborative research across bioengineering and neuroscience, and willingness to learn and train in the relevant topics.

Skills and Experience I Look For

  • Prior experience in experimental research projects.
  • Electronics background: intermediate experience with microcontrollers and analog/digital circuits, preferably including project experience beyond under- and post-graduate lab coursework.
  • Biomedical engineering background: hands-on experience with biomedical device instrumentation (amplifiers, filters, signal conditioning circuits) and basic biomedical signal processing.
  • At least beginner-level programming in Python and/or MATLAB.
  • Good to have, but not a hard requirement: hands-on experience in device fabrication or microfabrication.

What You Can Expect

  • Training and experience in conventional and thin-film microfabrication at world-class cleanroom facilities such as CeNSE.
  • Work at the frontier of neuromodulation therapies, and on making them achievable in practice by engineering implantable devices.
  • Opportunities to interact and build collaborations with clinicians, neurosurgeons and the surgical medical device industry.

About

Dr. Sagnik Middya completed his B.Tech (2017) in Electronics and Electrical Engineering at IIT Guwahati, where he focused on biosensors for point-of-care diagnostics. He then pursued an M.Res and PhD (2023) in bioelectronics at the University of Cambridge, developing conducting polymer-based multi-modal neural interfaces for neuroscience research. After his doctoral studies, Sagnik joined as an early employee at Coherence Neuro, a Cambridge-based startup specializing in neural interfaces with applications in therapy and management of brain and peripheral cancers. He is currently a postdoctoral researcher at the University of California, San Francisco, where his research centres on cancer neuroscience, investigating the interactions between neurons and brain tumours (glioma) and their impact on cancer progression.

He will join the Department of Instrumentation and Applied Physics (IAP) at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore, as an Assistant Professor in December 2026.
